Field notes from experiences designed to break you

The fastest team in a 36-hour race isn't always the fittest one. It's the one that doesn't turn on each other.

I've lived through the suffering to know what works.

Premise

Adventure racing stitches cycling, trekking, paddling, and navigation into one continuous event — thirty-six hours, four to a team, no sleep. Similar to life; sometimes it feels non-stop. You're working with people in the same struggle.

Character revealed

Hour nineteen. A hydro cut on the map read three miles. On the ground, with the switchbacks, it was closer to eleven. Every bend we thought was the last one wasn't, and I watched my team's spirit go with each false turn. Nobody was fighting. Nobody was talking, which is worse. I told them: "You gotta suffer through it if you want to tell the story." That was the whole intervention. It worked because it was true.

Creating future nostalgia

By hour twenty your legs stop asking your brain for permission. You'll say things you'd never say fresh, and primal instincts start to take over. This is where you need to focus on hope. Bring it forward and create that moment for others where they will look back and not only tell the story; but want to re-create that same experience for others.

Apathy outlasts ambition

Apathy and indifference are highly effective because they are low energy. All it needs is time. Cut bait with those people. Because you need to bring the fire in the gut. No one will give to you.

Your character in the story

I see the world with 3 players in the act: defendant, witness and jury. Those that do, those that watch and those that judge.
